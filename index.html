Not Today

A 30-second social film for an outdoor jacket made for people with asthma. Asthma taught her to say “not today.” This jacket is what makes saying yes feel safe.

Role Amy Wang, creative direction and AI workflow

Board FigJam

The problem and the insight

I wanted a social short for an outdoor jacket aimed at people with asthma. The product should help someone trust their body’s capacity to move, and feel better able to say yes to cold air, steep paths, and play.

For people with asthma, the fear of being out of breath is often what holds them back, more than the breath itself. The click we designed for

Three directions came out of that:

Direction 01

The Count

Quiet and personal. She counts steps the way people with asthma do, until the counting just stops at the top.

Direction 02 · chosen

Not Today

Rhythmic and a bit defiant. She holds back three times, then the same shots replay and she goes. Hard cut on the beat.

Direction 03

Lungs of the Mountain

Visual and poetic. Breath in step with the landscape. Soft, but less built for social sharing.

I picked Not Today. It has a built-in structure, a beat-synced turn that works with the sound off, and it shows the jacket moving.

03

Vizard asset pack

Before editing in Vizard I needed exact references: the girl from every angle, the jacket open and closed, details, props, and empty location plates. 33 FLUX stills and 6 labeled sheets.

04

10-second FLUX preview: v1 to v2

I generated a vertical 10-second cut with successive ~2s shots so I could check direction before the full film. Keyframes were pinned. Transitions were designed: match cut on the hand, snowball wipe, tilt, then a hard cut on the beat.

v1 self-critique

She never actually flung the door open. She wasn’t clearly zipping her collar. The “tilt” was a slide effect. The inhaler could read as a spray can.

v2 fixes

On the beat drop she shoves the door open into bright snow light. Hard cut to her gloved hand pulling the zip up to her chin. The inhaler is a blue plastic one.

05

Brand: alveo

From a list of ten names I chose alveo, from alveoli, the tiny air sacs in the lungs. It sounds Italian and premium to most people, and people with asthma catch the meaning.

Four logo directions. I picked 02 Circle, then refined it into 2A Refined, 2B Breath ring (the circle tapers like an exhale), and 2C Badge for small use.

Favorite lockup: 2B Breath ring on the back yoke. The ring quietly carries the asthma story.

06

Workflow test: Vizard vs direct BFL

I ran the same shot plan through Vizard and through direct Black Forest Labs video so the comparison was fair. Same assets, same 9:16 format, same timing. Score on consistency, transitions, whether the click hits, and time and cost.

The Vizard prompt calls for motivated transitions (match cuts, the snowball wipe, the tilt), not generic crossfades. Full prompt in process/prompts.md.

07

Story upgrade

The first cut was “she hesitates, then she goes,” but we never saw why she changes. Three fixes:

  1. A turning point. Her hand finds the inhaler, she breathes, she zips the collar up like putting on armor. Then the beat drops.
  2. About 20 shots at a faster social pace, with room for product close-ups.
  3. Brand close-ups that do story work (cuff, pocket, zip-pull badge, collar, hood, fabric, vents, back logo).

The visual thread is her hand on her chest, three times: as fear, as resolve on the zip, then as calm.

What I learned

  • Motivated transitions. Match cuts, the snowball wipe, and the tilt beat generic crossfades every time. I wrote that into the Vizard prompt on purpose.
  • Safety filters vs spelling. BFL’s filter blocked some logo and mark edits. Compositing the real vector mark with PIL kept “.alveo” exact on the jacket mockups.
  • Fair workflow tests. Comparing Vizard and direct BFL only works if the shot plan, assets, and format stay identical. Then score consistency, transitions, the click, and cost.
